# 1 · Collect, reconstruct, adapt

## Input

The user and their primary task; route and real content; existing product, brand, or system; owner-supplied images or URLs; decisions still open (for example, density, navigation, or how to reveal state).

## Work

1. Turn the brief into questions a reference can answer. Inspect the existing route and one neighboring surface before choosing what to carry forward. Place supplied references under the questions they answer.
2. Scan broadly by need. Use the [catalog](../references/catalog.md) to look for several candidates across comparable products and different source types: task sequences, component showcases, and visual compositions. Keep the scan lightweight: link, useful detail, and where it may not fit. Narrow to the strongest two to four patterns that solve different parts of the task. With parallel research, give each researcher a distinct decision and request links and observed details.
3. Copy what works **as a study**. Reconstruct the relevant slice at the level needed to understand it: trace a flow state by state, sketch its grid and type roles, or recreate one interaction from its showcase. Record the setup behind the result: slot structure, alignment, density, keyboard behavior, state changes, or motion. A still image supports a composition study; use a sequence or live surface for claims about interaction. Use the [pattern note](../references/pattern-note.md).
4. Adapt piece by piece. For each selected pattern, say what stays, what changes, and why: replace reference content, identity, and decorative assets with the project's; resize for actual content and widths; translate visual roles to the existing system, or name the token roles needed. Assemble one direction anchored in what makes the user's task easier. When references are inaccessible, identify the gap and study the closest project surface or a task-specific wireframe. Label the basis of each decision.

## Output

A short candidate list by question, selected pattern notes with evidence and adaptation decisions, and one sentence describing the direction and the intended visual priority. Hand off the chosen patterns plus the assumptions that the design contract must resolve.
