# The designer's workflow — researched

How strong brand, product and immersive-web studios actually take a project from brief to shipped site, read from their own process pages, case studies, build logs and interviews (fetched 2026-11-24). Every claim carries the page it came from. Quotes are verbatim; one source (Immersive Garden's Japanese interview on note.com) is an AI translation and is marked so. Where the sources do not support a common belief, the gap is stated instead of filled.

The visual board of this workflow, with credited frames from the studios, lives on the owner's private research share (`/wf/index.html`). The skill rewrite that follows from it: [v0.0.60-plan.md](v0.0.60-plan.md).

**Merged with the owner's research (2026-11-24).** The owner's [frontend design workflow research](owner/frontend_design_workflow_research_2026-11-24_en.md) is the primary input and is cited as [Owner §N]. It adds the product half (structure and real copy before visuals, states, a thin end-to-end slice, evaluation separated from generation, evidence types, completion criteria); this file keeps the studio half (principles from references, divergent directions, assets, motion language). What came from where, and the conflicts, are in [workflow-merge.md](workflow-merge.md).

## The workflow in one picture

```text
1 Frame (brief, words → observable requirements) ─► GATE: brief
   ─► 2 Structure & copy (IA, flows, real copy, grey browser prototype) ─► GATE: structure      [Owner §3, §4, §15.2]
   ─► 3 References → principles (deconstruction cards) ─► GATE: picks          (runs alongside 2)
   ─► 4 Directions on the same content (stylescape + representative screen + key interaction) ─► GATE: direction
   ─► 5 Visual language, assets & risk spike (key visuals, medium decision, hardest thing working) ─► GATE: key visuals + spike
   ─► 6 Design-system contract (tokens, type, components with states, imagery, motion tokens) ─► GATE: /system
   ─► 7 Motion language (role, storyboard, animatic, behavioural spec) ─► GATE: animatic + spec
   ─► 8 Composition map (hierarchy + asset + motion beat + states + responsive rules) ─► GATE: map
   ─► 9 Thin end-to-end slice (one core task, all states, real content) ─► GATE: slice in the browser   [Owner §15.5]
   ─► 10 Build-out ─► 11 Independent review & revision (separate agent, browser evidence) [Owner §13, §15.6–7]
   ─► 12 Final GATE, release, evidence ledger [Owner §15.8, §16]
```

The order is not a waterfall. Locomotive runs the technical spike (weeks 2–4) *before* art direction is frozen (weeks 3–6) — "This is where you test shader behavior, asset weight, touch input, loading, and fallback behavior before the visual direction becomes expensive to change." ([Locomotive, WebGL budget & timeline](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/product-launch-website-webgl-budget-timeline)). Ueno: "We don't have a 'big reveal' after squirreling away for weeks, but instead keep in touch often" ([Ueno, design process](https://medium.com/ueno/dear-ueno-whats-your-design-process-ee28e8e3dbfc)).

## Stage by stage

Each stage lists what goes in, the **visual artifact** that comes out, who decides, and the tools the studios name.

### 1. Frame: brief, audience and words

- **Owner rules for this stage:** record target users, core tasks, required content, brand direction, constraints and success criteria; translate vague words into observable requirements — "Replace vague requests such as 'make it premium' with observable requirements, such as typographic contrast, information density, the role of imagery, and the intensity of motion" — and label statements without user evidence as hypotheses [Owner §15 step 1], [Owner §3 stages 1–2]. For an existing service, read analytics, support inquiries and past research before new interviews [Owner §4].
- **Artifacts:** a collaborative brief that is re-agreed when it changes; a content inventory; a risk register and decision log; a definition of launch success ([Locomotive](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/product-launch-website-webgl-budget-timeline); [Ueno](https://medium.com/ueno/dear-ueno-whats-your-design-process-ee28e8e3dbfc)). Collins' Reposition programme lists a "Market & Competitive Intelligence Report" and "Vision Narratives" ([Collins, Reposition](https://wearecollins.com/programs/reposition/)).
- **The words that drive everything after:** stylescape references are collected against the client's words — "The first thing that you need is the words that describe what the client wants... you're going to collect images that represents those words" ([Ben Burns on Logo Geek](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/ben-burns/)).
- **Rules that matter for an agent:** "The first milestone is a signed scope, not a moodboard." "The brief should describe the visitor journey before it describes the technology." ([Locomotive](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/product-launch-website-webgl-budget-timeline)). Active Theory: "Our creative process comes after identifying and prioritising our clients' needs." ([Creativepool](https://creativepool.com/magazine/industry/introducing-the-transatlantic-emmy-winning-dream-weavers--companyspotlight.26977)).
- **Where the truth comes from:** interviews and the product itself, not documents. Instrument opened the Highwire sprint with a live product demo: "It gave us direct access to the emotional core of the brand in a way no briefing document could have." ([Instrument, Highwire Brand Sprint](https://www.instrument.com/latest/highwire-brand-sprint)). Immersive Garden: "involve the client to understand what they like and why" (AI-translated; [note.com interview](https://note.com/wgm/n/ne49806937c91?hl=en)).
- **Who decides:** the client signs the scope; Locomotive names a single decision-maker per approval ([Locomotive process](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/locomotive-website-project-timeline)).

### 2. Structure and real copy, before visuals (from the owner's research)

- **Artifacts:** sitemap and IA, user flows for the core tasks including error and recovery paths, actual copy and sample data, and "an inexpensive browser prototype with actual copy and sample data" that completes the goal "before polishing the appearance of the screens" [Owner §15 step 2], [Owner §3 stage 3].
- **The distinctions:** IA decides what things are called and how they relate; a flow connects actions and system responses toward a goal; a wireframe fixes what appears at each step and in what order; a prototype tests whether those decisions work [Owner §4].
- **Studio sources agree on the order:** "The brief should describe the visitor journey before it describes the technology." ([Locomotive](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/product-launch-website-webgl-budget-timeline)); "Make the story the priority over polished visuals" ([Koto](https://seasoned.koto.studio/book/delivering-the-goods/)).
- **Limit:** an AI persona or an agent completing the task does not show that real users understand it; keep a stage with real users, even a small one with hint-free tasks [Owner §4].

### 3. References → principles (not layouts)

- **Owner rule:** record "the principles you intend to adopt" — heading/body contrast, density, page rhythm, role of imagery, button emphasis, mobile changes, speed and purpose of motion — "rather than reproducing an entire site's appearance" [Owner §5].
- **Artifacts:** a wide, undecided reference sweep; moodboards; a standing personal library. Koto: "Rather than making any decisions, we start by looking for references and exploring lots of different visual mechanics." ([Koto, Seasoned](https://seasoned.koto.studio/book/making-your-ideas-a-reality/)). Active Theory's CD: "Most of the time I'm collecting and sorting reference material into buckets for projects" ([Creativepool](https://creativepool.com/magazine/industry/introducing-the-transatlantic-emmy-winning-dream-weavers--companyspotlight.26977)). Lusion's first step is a moodboard of the product's patterns, colours and history plus a list of other artists' images and video ([Lusion, concept to production](https://medium.com/lusion-ltd/from-concept-prototyping-to-production-in-a-creative-studio-f2083e96c4b9)).
- **Historical deconstruction is a named step:** "At COLLINS, every project begins with deep reconnaissance into design history—understanding the aesthetics, cultural forces and philosophies that shaped pivotal works." ([Collins, Institute of Design](https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/institute-of-design/)). The output there is a rule (six geometric modules that transform into words and images), not a restyled surface.
- See [How references are used](#how-references-are-used) for the mechanics.

### 4. Concept directions and stylescapes

- **Owner rule (wins on form):** "create a small number of distinct directions using the same content"; compare "different typography, density, composition, and approaches to imagery, rather than changing colors alone"; limit scope "so a choice can be made from one representative screen and the key interaction"; after the pick, "record the reasons for the choice and the core rules" [Owner §15 step 3]. Apply styles "to key screens containing actual content" first [Owner §5]. Merged artifact: per direction, a stylescape strip + stage 2's representative screen in real copy + a short key-interaction sketch.
- **How many:** the only hard numbers are from brand studios. Instrument explored "three distinct territories and stretched them as far apart as possible: different typographic sensibilities, photographic logic, color philosophies" ([Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/latest/highwire-brand-sprint)). Blind (Ben Burns): "We'll do three that are based on what we think that the client wants, and then we'll throw one more in there" — and "maybe five or six times out of 10 they'll choose the wild card" ([Logo Geek](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/ben-burns/)). Ueno: "a couple of concepts emerge as the ones we take forward" ([Ueno](https://medium.com/ueno/dear-ueno-whats-your-design-process-ee28e8e3dbfc)).
- **Immersive studios differ:** none of the seven immersive studios read publishes a direction count or uses stylescapes; Studio Freight's client describes "a massive collection of different, high-fidelity concepts" ([Studio Freight, info](https://studiofreight.com/info)); Basement and Active Theory describe brainstorm and daily critique sessions ([Webby, KidSuper World](https://www.webbyawards.com/basement-studio-kidsuper-world/); [Creativepool](https://creativepool.com/magazine/industry/introducing-the-transatlantic-emmy-winning-dream-weavers--companyspotlight.26977)). Collins went with one concept expressed as a generative rule ([Collins](https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/institute-of-design/)). The "2–3 directions" rule is brand-design practice; it is adopted here because the owner asked for it and because it is the cheap place to diverge.
- **The stylescape artifact:** coined at Blind by Chris Do and José Caballer; "we just want to move past a Pinterest board and into something that's more like a composition because the key to this thing is being able to see how different pieces work together." One wide canvas (about 55 × 15 in printed, walked through as roughly three 1920×1080 views) showing typography, wayfinding, colour, iconography, UI and interiors — touchpoints, not just pictures. "I can do one in four to six hours." A real customer photo from discovery is placed in the board so the client can judge whether it is their buyer. ([Logo Geek](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/ben-burns/)). Brightscout: "Think of them as a more curated mood board"; "Usually, Stylescapes are presented in a couple of different options" ([Brightscout](https://www.brightscout.com/insight/collection-of-the-best-brand-stylescapes-ever-created)).
- **How the pick is made:** criteria, not a vote — "Which ones best reflect the brand idea? Is there a unified system emerging? Does it strike the tone you're going for? Are you excited by it?" ([Koto](https://seasoned.koto.studio/book/making-your-ideas-a-reality/)). Divergence is meant to shrink the space: "once they choose a Stylescape, the sandbox that you have to plan gets a lot smaller" ([Logo Geek](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/ben-burns/)).
- **Gate mechanics:** Instrument's workshops each "focused on a specific element like type, color, or photography and functioned as a decision session rather than a presentation", and the anti-pattern is "The endless rounds of 'could we see one more direction.'" ([Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/latest/highwire-brand-sprint)).
- **Generated imagery at this stage is studio practice:** OFF+BRAND used Midjourney to converge on art direction for Aether 1 before modelling ([Codrops, Aether 1](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/08/06/building-aether-1-sound-without-boundaries/)); Instrument uses AI to "rapidly prototype ideas" and then curates hard ([Instrument, taste](https://www.instrument.com/latest/taste-is-the-new-competitive-advantage)).

### 5. Visual language, assets, the medium decision and the risk spike

- **Owner rules:** validate risks before detailed design — "Experiment with complex inputs, transitions, 3D, and responsive behavior"; minimum output "Small working prototype"; move on when "The core experience is feasible in the target environment" [Owner §3 stage 5]. Real-time 3D is justified when users change viewpoints or interact with options and scene states; "If the goal is only to show a predetermined scene, a rendered image or video may suffice" [Owner §12]. 3D pipeline: concept → blockout → asset creation → look-dev → handoff (glTF/GLB) → web integration → optimisation → alternative experiences; measure transfer size and rendering cost separately [Owner §12]. Stripe could only assess its globe once "a working initial prototype had been created" [Owner §12, R41].
- **Decide what 3D is for, per moment:** "If the concept includes 3D or WebGL, this is where we decide what it's for and how fast it has to run." ([Locomotive process](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/locomotive-website-project-timeline)); "not every moment needs a canvas" ([Locomotive budget](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/product-launch-website-webgl-budget-timeline)); 3D must answer a communication problem ([Locomotive, what three.js developers solve](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/what-three-js-developers-solve-with-3d)). Resn chose *not* to use WebGL for Zentry: "Instead of relying on external 3-D graphic libraries, we focused on using memory-efficient DOM technologies" ([Communication Arts, Zentry](https://www.commarts.com/project/37553/zentry)).
- **Art direction by subtraction:** "We spent more time removing ideas than creating new ones, keeping only what was most relevant." ([Communication Arts, Immersive Garden](https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/immersive-garden)).
- **The look sets the budget:** Basement's PS2 direction — "what pixel ratio feels authentic? How low can we go with textures? How many polygons are too many? Should we add dithering—and if so, where?" ([Basement, new digital HQ](https://basement.studio/post/new-digital-hq-pt-1)).
- **What a visual language contains** (Pentagram, SSG): mark, typeface, colour system with pairings, "A library of graphic shapes, iconography that plays with line weight on an extensible unit grid", pattern library, computational photo treatment, infographic library, 3D logo animation, a unified storytelling method ([Pentagram, SSG](https://www.pentagram.com/work/sustainability-solutions-group/story)). Further derived Trustpilot's entire layout language from the star rating: its "proportions and square geometry became the foundation for an entire visual language" ([Further, Trustpilot](https://www.further.group/work/trustpilot)).
- **Imagery rules are part of the contract:** IBM names three photo types with distinct jobs — reportage ("The bulk of the photography we use is lifestyle, cinematic and colorful"), portraiture and still life ([IBM Design Language, photography](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/photography/overview/)).
- **3D production artifacts:** OFF+BRAND — Midjourney for direction → Figma front/side/back iteration → a storyboard matching each scene to a 3D visualisation → "we created a full-motion version of it" → UV unwrap and "rename all parts and organize the file so the developer can easily recognize which object is which" → baked .glb with AO, fixed camera paths, 60 FPS on an iPhone SE 2020 as the acceptance bar ([Codrops, Aether 1](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/08/06/building-aether-1-sound-without-boundaries/)). Resn "opted for unlit materials in the whole experience. This meant we had to bake lighting and shadows in all textures." ([Resn, Banditø](https://medium.com/resn/banditø-immersive-experience-f258361dead3)). Immersive Garden bakes six relief light levels into channel-packed textures ([teardown](https://rickcreates.com/r/2025-01-07-immersive-garden)). Basement's single global shader "was used by nearly every element in the scene" ([Basement](https://basement.studio/post/new-digital-hq-pt-1)). Studio Freight "built a custom tool using WebGL shaders which allowed us to use pretty much any file type for image generation and manipulation" ([Awwwards, Dragonfly](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-dragonfly-by-studio-freight.html)).
- **Tools named by the studios:** Houdini, Cinema 4D, Redshift, Three.js (Lusion, [Communication Arts](https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/lusion)); Blender, C4D, ZBrush, Houdini, After Effects, three.js, GSAP, Lenis (Immersive Garden, [Communication Arts](https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/immersive-garden)); Blender, ZBrush, Substance Painter, DaVinci Resolve, After Effects ([Basement](https://basement.studio/post/behind-the-ship)); Next.js, r3f, Three.js, GSAP, Lenis ([Awwwards, Dragonfly](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-dragonfly-by-studio-freight.html)).
- **Prototyping is part of design:** "Prototyping is design." Two questions: "Can we build this?" / "Does this feel right?"; "Prototypes should be cheap and disposable" ([Instrument, why we prototype](https://www.instrument.com/latest/why-we-prototype)). Lusion's "Concept Prototyping" lays out "the foundations of what we will be creating for our client", and its process includes a section titled "Learn to Give Up" for effects that fail on performance ([Lusion](https://medium.com/lusion-ltd/from-concept-prototyping-to-production-in-a-creative-studio-f2083e96c4b9)). Active Theory: "we initially were only going to include three 3D environments and in the end created eight environments instead" ([Webby, Active Theory](https://www.webbyawards.com/crafted-with-code/active-theory/)).

### 6. The design-system contract

- **Owner rules:** three layers — base values, semantic tokens, component tokens "where needed" — then components, patterns and governance [Owner §7]. Start small: "recurring semantic tokens, typography, containers, and basic components"; agents "look for an existing component before creating a new one"; one owner for tokens and shared APIs [Owner §15 step 4]. **States** are part of the contract: buttons (default, hover, focus-visible, pressed, disabled, processing), inputs, lists and search (loading, no results, one, many, partial failure), data cards, saving, permissions, modals (Escape, focus entry and return) [Owner §5]. Storybook shows components "with different props, states, and data", with interaction, visual and accessibility checks attached [Owner §7]; the owner chose it on 2026-11-24 (Discord), so the `/system` gate is a Storybook build with one story per state — see [v0.0.60-plan.md](v0.0.60-plan.md) item 11. Do not copy token values into prose; document intent and link to the generated values [Owner §14].
- **Tokens are role-based and binding.** Carbon: "Tokens are role-based, and themes specify the color values that serve those roles in the UI." "Unlike hex codes, tokens apply universally across themes." Interaction states are numeric (hover half a step, active two steps, selected one) and focus "must pass 3:1 color contrast" ([Carbon, colour](https://carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/color/overview)). Atlassian: token name = foundation + property + modifier; "Choose tokens based on meaning where applicable, not specific values."; "Don't use a token just because the colors appear to match." ([Atlassian, design tokens](https://atlassian.design/foundations/tokens/design-tokens)).
- **Guidelines are rule + example + misuse.** Mound's seven sections (story, logo, colour, typography, imagery, voice, applications); one page = rule, picture, wrong example, asset link; "Rules that are too strict push people to ignore them. Say where flexibility is allowed."; "When designers hand work to developers, the brand rules become tokens and styles." ([Mound, brand guidelines](https://mound.studio/newsletter/brand-guidelines-what-to-include)).
- **Motion belongs to the system gate.** Locomotive's design-system phase covers "typography, colour, components, responsive behaviour and motion" ([Locomotive process](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/locomotive-website-project-timeline)).
- **Type is infrastructure.** Pentagram commissioned a variable grotesque for SSG; Further built Trustpilot Sans ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/sustainability-solutions-group/story); [Further](https://www.further.group/work/trustpilot)). Kris Sowersby's order for a custom face — sketch n, a, g, p; build out lowercase; test in words; caps with "HAMBURGERFONTSIV" — and "you usually only need to put a pinch of flavour in each letter... a typeface is a functional thing after all" ([Klim](https://klim.co.nz/blog/computer-arts-interview/)). For an agent this becomes type exploration with real copy, not custom type design.
- **Pressure-tested before handoff:** Collins "Validated Versatility": "We pressure test our designs across every touchpoint and context—from app tiles to billboards, screens to swag." ([Collins](https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/institute-of-design/)). Mound: "If the guide says the primary colour is one blue and the product uses another, people will trust neither." ([Mound](https://mound.studio/newsletter/brand-guidelines-what-to-include)).

### 7. Motion language, storyboard, animatic and behavioural spec

Motion is designed at four layers: principles, tokens, a brand motion language, and storyboard/animatic. Studios present it running, not described.

- **Owner sequence:** define the role of motion first (state change, relationship, story, brand character), then: motion brief → states and triggers → storyboard → timing study → technical experiment on the hardest transition in the target browser → specification and integration (duration, easing or spring, sequence, interruption, mobile, reduced motion) → real-world checks (fast scroll, repeated clicks, back navigation, resize, touch, keyboard) [Owner §11]. A video mockup helps agree on timing "but it does not implement actual input, routing, or asynchronous states" [Owner §11], so the gate pairs the animatic with a behavioural spec written like the owner's search-panel example (focus moves to the input, Escape returns focus, input mid-transition reverses from the current state, reduced motion removes large movement, results do not wait for motion) [Owner §11]. Avoid two animation engines on the same element [Owner §11]. For ITSACHAR-type sites, concentrate expressive motion in one or two signature sequences [Owner §16].
- **Principles first (the "why").** Fluent 2: Functional / Natural / Consistent / Appealing ([Fluent 2, motion](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/motion)). Atlassian: "Motion is a clarifying layer, not decoration." ([Atlassian, motion](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion)). Apple: "Add motion purposefully, supporting the experience without overshadowing it." ([Apple HIG, motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)). Zajno fixed three animation principles before building: "when planning a project, it's crucial to define key principles early on" ([Codrops, Green Stack](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/03/26/design-in-motion-the-animation-principles-behind-green-stack/)).
- **Two registers.** Carbon splits all motion into productive and expressive: "Reserve expressive motion for occasional, important moments, to better capture the user's attention and offer a rhythmic break to the productive experience." ([Carbon, motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/motion/overview)). Atlassian's frequency test: "If someone will trigger this motion dozens of times a day, keep it under 150ms. If they see it once a session, you have room for more expression." and "Make exit motion faster than entrances" ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion)).
- **Tokens (the spec).**

  | System | Easing | Durations |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Carbon | standard `cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0.38,0.9)`, entrance `(0,0,0.38,0.9)`, exit `(0.2,0,1,0.9)`, plus expressive variants | 70 / 110 / 150 / 240 / 400 / 700 ms; duration scales with element size and distance |
  | Material 3 | emphasized `(0.2,0,0,1)`, emphasized-accelerate `(0.3,0,0.8,0.15)`, emphasized-decelerate `(0.05,0.7,0.1,1)` | short 50–200, medium 250–400, long 450–600, extra-long 700–1000 ms |
  | Atlassian | ease-out bold `(0,0.4,0,1)`, ease-in-out bold `(0.4,0,0,1)`, practical in/out | interactions 50–150, transitions 150–400 ms |

  Sources: [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/motion/overview); Material 3 values from the official token file ([material-web `_md-sys-motion.scss`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-web/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss), because m3.material.io rendered no text); [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion). Easing is semantic in Carbon: exits accelerate out because the departure is permanent, while a side panel that stays nearby uses standard easing.
- **Choreography.** "Staggering is the process of delaying the start of one or more animations"; "Give important elements more emphasis through more prominent movements and longer durations." ([Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/motion)). "Lead with one clear focal point… Don't run multiple simultaneous animations that pull attention in different directions." ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion)).
- **Brand motion language is derived, not decorated.** HSBC (Flump): "the idea of opening became the core starting point, acting as the mechanic that would introduce every animated asset in the brand" ([Flump, HSBC](https://www.flumpstudio.com/projects/hsbc-motion-t4wd9)). COLLINS, SF Symphony: "The motion language borrows from musical structure: visuals build like crescendos, pause like rests, and transition like key changes." ([COLLINS](https://wearecollins.com/story/sfsymphony/)). COLLINS, OFFF: exactly two named behaviours, Attraction and Projection; the client went from redesigning every year to "a constant, but adaptive design system" ([COLLINS, OFFF](https://wearecollins.com/story/offf/)). Koto's Replay mark "is dynamic and regenerating" ([Koto, Replay](https://koto.com/projects/replay)). Immersive Garden (AI-translated): "there is no project that started with the intention of incorporating motion design from the beginning" — motion follows the story ([note.com](https://note.com/wgm/n/ne49806937c91?hl=en)).
- **Motion as a handed-over system.** MOWE's Tectonic delivered "a 40-page motion guide", 20+ reusable motion assets and 9 templates, with Storyboard and Script Support as separately scoped items, so the client team could work "often without the involvement of a dedicated motion designer" ([MOWE, Tectonic](https://mowe.studio/work/tectonic-motion-system/)). Athletics built Nike's documentary motion kit (titles, transitions, lower thirds, closing tags) and showed **two** title directions; the client chose the systemic one "favouring the consistency and clarity it brought" ([The Brand Identity, Athletics × Nike](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/how-athletics-designed-a-flexible-motion-system-for-nikes-internal-north-american-documentaries)).
- **Storyboard → animatic → sign-off.** Resn: "we created a rough storyboard to define the timeline and the mood of the five chapters we would build." ([Resn](https://medium.com/resn/banditø-immersive-experience-f258361dead3)). Basement: concept art → "blocking, translating the animatic into 3D space, animating cameras, understanding scale, and mapping out what needed to be built" → master keys → animatic and prerender passes → grade ([Basement, Ship](https://basement.studio/post/behind-the-ship)). OFF+BRAND made "a full-motion version" before build so everyone agrees on flow, look and feel ([Codrops, Aether 1](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/08/06/building-aether-1-sound-without-boundaries/)). Roman Jean-Elie recorded work-in-progress videos and found "what I initially thought would be the centerpiece almost became optional" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/11/27/letting-the-creative-process-shape-a-webgl-portfolio/)).
- **Prototype is the spec.** Jitter's site: "all micro-interactions were designed in Jitter… eliminated a lot of the back-and-forth in the handoff process", exported as Lottie; developers used the same tool, "motion as a shared language, not a handoff artifact" ([Codrops, Jitter](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/09/02/a-behind-the-scenes-look-at-the-new-jitter-website/)). GSAP's timeline is "a container for Tweens" with position parameters and labels — a named, seekable spec rather than a video ([GSAP docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/)). Figma Smart Animate silently falls back to "a default dissolve transition" for unsupported properties ([Figma help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360039818874-Smart-animate-layers-between-frames)). Rive ships logic: the state machine is "the brain of any Rive project" ([Rive, data binding](https://rive.app/blog/getting-started-with-data-binding)). Lottie handoff is an explicit export step with format, platform and player ([LottieFiles](https://help.lottiefiles.com/hc/en-us/articles/8704650653593-Handoff-Embed)).
- **Motion QA checklist.** Carbon: purposeful ("What problem is motion solving?"), responsive ("Do micro-interactions fall within a static duration ranging from 90–120 ms?"), meticulous, unobtrusive — "The best interface motion may go unnoticed" ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/motion/overview)). Apple: "Let people cancel motion." and avoid sustained oscillation around 0.2 Hz ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)).
- **Reduced motion is a requirement.** WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA): "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential to the functionality or the information being conveyed." ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). MDN's example replaces a scale pulse with a dissolve ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)). Gate animation behind `no-preference` and listen to `matchMedia` changes to stop running JS animation ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)). Motion for React `reducedMotion="user"` disables transform and layout animations while keeping opacity and colour ([Motion](https://motion.dev/docs/react-accessibility)). What triggers vestibular problems: large relative movement, mismatched direction or speed (parallax, scrolljacking), perceived distance; "Animation that involves only non-moving properties, like opacity, color, and blurs, are unlikely to be problematic." ([A List Apart, Val Head](https://alistapart.com/article/designing-safer-web-animation-for-motion-sensitivity/)).

### 8. Composition: the page as a sequence

- **Owner rules:** responsive design is "a set of rules", not three mockups — when columns change, navigation collapses, cards wrap; check long titles, enlarged text, missing images, font-load shifts, no hover, virtual keyboard, landscape and zoom; mobile keeps the information and tasks and may drop effects (Design Embraced used WebGL on desktop, CSS/JS on mobile) [Owner §10]. Each section's states come from the stage 6 table [Owner §5].
- **Scroll narrative as a stage map with weights:** Lusion v3 splits the astronaut journey into nine stages with explicit scroll weights ([teardown](https://rickcreates.com/r/2023-10-02-lusion-v3)). Resn's five chapters map to song sections, and interaction semantics are decided at storyboard level ([Resn](https://medium.com/resn/banditø-immersive-experience-f258361dead3)).
- **Story before decoration:** "Sometimes we intentionally remove as much decoration as possible to tell the story." (Immersive Garden, AI-translated; [note.com](https://note.com/wgm/n/ne49806937c91?hl=en)). Koto presents the story first: "Make the story the priority over polished visuals – they don't need to be perfect yet." and mocks up real placements ([Koto, delivering the goods](https://seasoned.koto.studio/book/delivering-the-goods/)).

### 9. Thin end-to-end slice (from the owner's research)

- **Artifact:** one core task built end to end in the chosen direction, with its normal, loading, empty, failure and permission states, real content, responsive rules and its motion integrated — "Building one complete, thin end-to-end slice first reduces the chance of discovering integration problems only after producing a large number of static screens" [Owner §15 step 5]. Implementation order inside it: shared structure → key components with realistic data shapes → the task across screens → errors, empty values, back navigation, refresh, direct links → responsive, keyboard, touch, focus → motion, media and 3D tied to loading and state [Owner §8].
- **Gate:** the owner uses the slice in the browser. Prototype code adopted for production is re-checked against the same quality criteria [Owner §15 step 5].

### 10. Build-out

- **Owner rules:** a handoff needs component mappings, actual copy, assets, state transitions, responsive rules, focus and keyboard policies, motion and mobile alternatives and acceptance criteria [Owner §8]. Decide whether a CMS or content model is needed from how often content changes and who edits it; a small developer-edited site does not need one upfront [Owner §8]. Parallel agents work on independent screens; one integration owner re-checks shared tokens and components, because separate worktrees "do not resolve contradictions in design decisions" [Owner §15].
- **Common architecture:** one full-screen canvas behind the DOM; the DOM owns layout and text, JS reads DOM boxes and draws WebGL planes at the same coordinates (Lusion v3, [teardown](https://rickcreates.com/r/2023-10-02-lusion-v3)); one canvas via tunnel-rat with per-section rendering only in view (Studio Freight, [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-dragonfly-by-studio-freight.html)); per-page render targets composited with bloom and FXAA (Immersive Garden, [teardown](https://rickcreates.com/r/2025-01-07-immersive-garden)).
- **Designers and developers overlap from concept:** Studio Freight brought developers into the concept phase for Dragonfly ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-dragonfly-by-studio-freight.html)). Active Theory built Hydra so artists build scenes in the browser while developers script nodes ([Active Theory](https://medium.com/active-theory/the-story-of-technology-built-at-active-theory-5d17ae0e3fb4)).

### 11. Independent review and revision

- **Owner rules:** separate generation from evaluation — the review agent "opens the browser and checks behavior, screenshots, responsiveness, keyboard operation, and errors" and records specific findings with evidence; the evaluator stays lenient and limited on aesthetics, so human judgment remains [Owner §15 step 6, R51]. Keep seven review types apart: visual, functional, responsive, accessibility, motion, performance, operational [Owner §13]. Playwright visual comparisons are regression checks against an approved baseline — "They do not judge whether the baseline itself is good design" — and baselines are not overwritten on every run [Owner §13]. Core Web Vitals good thresholds: LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at p75 of real visits, mobile and desktop separately; a single Lighthouse run is not user performance; for 3D also watch frame delays, memory and heat [Owner §13]. Each revision names the element, the user effect and the expected state; keep good states, cap iterations, do not assume the last version is best [Owner §15 step 7].
- "Performance is a design constraint, not a cleanup task." Device matrix, asset budget cap with a named owner, reduced-motion and non-WebGL paths are scope items; QA ends in a go/no-go on test results ([Locomotive budget](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/product-launch-website-webgl-budget-timeline); [Locomotive process](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/locomotive-website-project-timeline)).
- **Counter-evidence to copy deliberately:** award-winning sites in this set fail basic accessibility — Immersive Garden and Lusion v3 ignore `prefers-reduced-motion` and have broken keyboard/focus paths ([IG teardown](https://rickcreates.com/r/2025-01-07-immersive-garden); [Lusion teardown](https://rickcreates.com/r/2023-10-02-lusion-v3)). The skill keeps its accessibility QA; it does not inherit this blind spot.
- **Gap:** no studio primary source describes a formal post-build fidelity audit as its own discipline; the evidence covers pre-handoff completeness (Mound), cross-touchpoint pressure tests (Collins) and performance bars (OFF+BRAND, Locomotive).

### 12. Presentation, approval gates, release and the evidence ledger

- **Owner rules:** record "an agent's successful task completion, passing automated checks, human visual approval, and validation with real users as different kinds of evidence"; deploy after the necessary review, then use usage and error data for the next change [Owner §15 step 8]. The final completion criteria are the ten in [Owner §16] (core tasks complete; normal and realistic exception paths; layout holds with real, long and empty content; consistent type, spacing, colour and components; readable at representative devices and intermediate widths; keyboard, focus, labels, reduced motion reviewed; media, 3D and motion do not block main actions or performance; preview vs deployment differences checked; content update, error response and rollback exist; AI evaluations and real-user evidence recorded separately). Operational items: Korean and mixed-language content, publishing ownership, SEO, asset rights, task events, consent, rollback [Owner §13].
- **Explain, don't reveal:** "Good teams don't just present work, they explain it. They adapt to the client's level of experience, connect every decision to a business goal" ([Koto, rebrands get judged in seconds](https://koto.com/latest/rebrands-get-judged-in-seconds)).
- **Show it running:** Lusion invites clients "to view their product in real-time animation [...] If we get the ALL OK from our client, then we can pass the product over." ([Lusion](https://medium.com/lusion-ltd/from-concept-prototyping-to-production-in-a-creative-studio-f2083e96c4b9)). Immersive Garden: "When sharing the image of the output with the client at the concept phase stage, we create prototypes and videos." (AI-translated; [note.com](https://note.com/wgm/n/ne49806937c91?hl=en)).
- **One voice for feedback:** Locomotive asks for one owner of consolidated feedback — "a long chain of separate comments is a schedule risk" ([Locomotive budget](https://locomotive.ca/en/blog/product-launch-website-webgl-budget-timeline)).
- **Named approval:** "Once's the visual and verbal identity is approved with the client - it's time to deliver the goods." ([Koto](https://seasoned.koto.studio/book/delivering-the-goods/)).
- **Deliverable list as the contract:** Highwire's sprint delivered "strategy, tone of voice, messaging pillars, logo, color, typography, photography direction, a mocked-up landing page, and brand guidelines." ([Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/latest/highwire-brand-sprint)).

## How references are used

The studios translate references; they do not restyle them.

1. **Collect against words, compose into a claim.** Images are gathered for the client's words and applied across touchpoints, then composed so the board shows "how different pieces work together"; mixed-source images are re-graded into one artifact; the attorney-checked line is "as long as you're not selling this stuff as a deliverable or directly copying them in the final result" ([Logo Geek](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/ben-burns/)).
2. **Recombine, don't select.** "Mix, match elements of your moodboards to create something original and distinctive." ([Koto](https://seasoned.koto.studio/book/making-your-ideas-a-reality/)).
3. **Extract a rule.** Studio Freight looked at "the title sequences of directors like Scorsese and Tarantino, where the type is treated as seriously as anything else on screen" and took the rule, not a layout ([Codrops, Studio Freight](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/29/studio-freight-moving-missions-forward/)). Collins turned Bauhaus history into six transforming modules ([Collins](https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/institute-of-design/)).
4. **Rebuild the effect in your own system.** Lusion's moodboard held a Robert Hodgin clip and scanned models; the team then wrote its own physics and SDF pipeline ([Lusion](https://medium.com/lusion-ltd/from-concept-prototyping-to-production-in-a-creative-studio-f2083e96c4b9)).
5. **Invert a known source.** Resn rebuilt environments fans knew from Twenty One Pilots videos, then desaturated them and added fog for the dark→light arc ([Resn](https://medium.com/resn/banditø-immersive-experience-f258361dead3)).
6. **Use a reference as a constraint.** Basement's PS2 era is a budget: its limitations "actually helped us keep things lightweight and fast on the web" ([Basement](https://basement.studio/post/new-digital-hq-pt-1)).
7. **Refuse the template.** Lusion: "the best ideas usually fall apart the moment you force them into a template" ([Codrops, Lusion](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/13/lusion-where-digital-craft-meets-ambitious-experimentation/)); Studio Freight: "brutal elegance [...] dropping good ideas for one great one" ([Codrops, Studio Freight](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/29/studio-freight-moving-missions-forward/)).

For the skill this means a **deconstruction card** per reference: the principle (what it does and why it works), the axis it informs (composition, type, colour, imagery, motion, interaction), and an explicit "not carried" line naming the layout and signature elements that stay with the source.

- Immersive Garden quotes come from an AI translation of a Japanese interview.
- No conference talk transcripts or FWA pages were read; The Futur's stylescape episodes are video-only and were not watched.
